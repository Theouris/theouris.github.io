Circle of Willis and arterial branches

  • Internal carotid → ACA/MCA; vertebral → PICA; vertebrals unite as basilar.
  • Basilar → pontine arteries, AICA, SCA, and terminal PCA; ACOM and PCOM connections.

Original slide 5/23

Image-only cranial nerve nuclei chart

  • Motor medial/sensory lateral to sulcus limitans; midbrain, pons, medulla, spinal extension.
  • GSE: III/IV midbrain, VI pons, XII medulla.
  • SVE/branchial motor: motor V and facial VII in pons, ambiguus IX/X in medulla, spinal accessory XI in cervical cord.
  • GVE: Edinger-Westphal III, superior salivatory VII, inferior salivatory IX, dorsal motor X.
  • SVA/GVA: solitary nucleus receives VII/IX/X, taste chiefly rostral and visceral input caudal.
  • GSA: mesencephalic V proprioception, principal V touch/vibration, spinal V pain-temperature with small VII/IX/X contributions.
  • SSA: vestibular/cochlear VIII at pontomedullary levels. Traditional cranial-XI/ambiguus terminology is distinct from spinal XI to SCM/trapezius.

Original slide 6/23

Four-step brainstem localization

  • Cranial deficit → level: III/IV midbrain; V-VIII pons/pontomedullary; IX/X/XII medulla.
  • Body deficit → corticospinal, medial lemniscus, or spinothalamic; then medial/lateral, then vascular territory.

Original extracted slide text
Brainstem Localization: A Four-Step Approach
1. Cranial nerve deficit → LEVEL
• Midbrain → III, IV
• Pons → V–VIII
• Medulla → IX, X, XII
2. Body deficit → LONG TRACT
• Weakness → corticospinal
• Vibration/proprioception → medial lemniscus
• Pain/temperature → spinothalamic
3. Determine LOCATION
• Medial vs lateral
4. Identify VASCULAR TERRITORY
• Match the involved structures to the artery
CN → Level | Body → Tract | Medial/Lateral → Location | Artery → Syndrome
Original slide 7/23

Crossed brainstem findings

  • Ipsilateral cranial nucleus/fascicle or pre-crossing face sensory dysfunction plus contralateral body long-tract findings.
  • Ipsilateral face pain-temperature loss with opposite body pain-temperature loss is a key example; crossing and nuclear exceptions still matter.
Show original slide image
Original extracted slide text
The Crossed Brainstem Pattern
Brainstem Lesions: Face vs Body
• Cranial nerve nuclei/fascicles affect the ipsilateral head/face
• Long tracts may produce contralateral body deficits
• Therefore, brainstem lesions commonly produce crossed neurological findings
Example
• Ipsilateral facial pain/temperature loss
• Contralateral body pain/temperature loss
→ Brainstem lesion
Brainstem Lesion
Ipsilateral cranial nerve/face deficit + contralateral body deficit → think BRAINSTEM
Berkowitz. Clinical Neurology and Neuroanatomy. McGraw Hill.
Original slide 8/23

Medial versus lateral anatomy

  • Medial: corticospinal, medial lemniscus, MLF, and selected motor cranial structures.
  • Lateral: spinothalamic, spinal V, cerebellar connections, descending sympathetics, selected sensory/autonomic nuclei.

Original extracted slide text
Medial vs Lateral Localization
Medial
• Corticospinal tract → motor
• Medial lemniscus → vibration/proprioception
• MLF → conjugate eye movements
• Medially located motor CN nuclei/fascicles
Lateral
• Spinothalamic tract → body pain/temperature
• Spinal trigeminal system → facial pain/temperature
• Cerebellar pathways → coordination
• Descending sympathetic fibers → sympathetic function
• Selected sensory/autonomic CN nuclei
Central Medulla
Medial → motor + medial lemniscus | Lateral → pain/temp + cerebellar/autonomic

Original slide 9/23

Horner syndrome

  • Miosis from dilator loss, mild ptosis from superior tarsal denervation, variable anhidrosis from sweat-gland sympathetic interruption.
  • Descending hypothalamospinal fibers travel in lateral brainstem; central lesion produces ipsilateral signs. Distinguish from III levator weakness and mydriasis.

Horner Syndrome
Cause: Interruption of sympathetic innervation to the head
Classic Findings
• Miosis → loss of pupillary dilator
• Mild ptosis → loss of superior tarsal muscle
• Anhidrosis → loss of sympathetic innervation to sweat glands
Brainstem Localization
• Descending hypothalamospinal sympathetic fibers travel
through the lateral brainstem
• Lateral brainstem lesion → ipsilateral Horner syndrome

Original slide 13/23

Lateral medullary / Wallenberg syndrome

  • Ambiguus → dysphagia/hoarseness; spinal V → ipsilateral face pain-temperature; spinothalamic → opposite body pain-temperature.
  • Inferior cerebellar peduncle → ipsilateral ataxia; vestibular → vertigo/nystagmus; sympathetics → ipsilateral Horner.
  • PICA or vertebral territory; major corticospinal and medial lemniscal deficits are not required in the classic lateral pattern.

Lateral pons versus lateral medulla

Weber syndrome

  • Ventromedial midbrain: III fascicle → ipsilateral III palsy; corticospinal → opposite limb weakness; corticobulbar → opposite lower-face weakness.
  • PCA branches are the classic association; distinguish from dorsal midbrain compression and tegmental ataxic/tremor patterns.

Original slide 21/23

Dorsal midbrain / Parinaud syndrome

  • Dorsal/pretectal lesion → impaired upgaze, light-near dissociation, convergence-retraction movements.
  • Often pineal-region mass compression; this is different from an isolated peripheral III palsy or Weber motor pattern.

Original extracted slide text
Dorsal Midbrain (Parinaud) Syndrome
• Location: dorsal midbrain / pretectal region
• Impaired upward gaze (vertical gaze palsy)
• Pupils constrict to near, but not to light (light-near dissociation)
• Convergence-retraction nystagmus
• Often associated with compression by a pineal region mass
Vertical gaze abnormality → dorsal midbrain
Midbrain – superior colliculus level, Waxman, 28e.
Original slide 22/23

Integrated localization sequence

  • Cranial nerve → level; body modality → long tract; structure combination → medial/lateral; territory → artery; only then syndrome name.
  • Use anatomical explanation rather than requiring every patient to display a complete textbook triad.

Original extracted slide text
Brainstem Localization
1. Find the cranial nerve deficit → LEVEL
2. Find the body deficit → LONG TRACT
3. Combine the structures → MEDIAL vs LATERAL
4. Identify the vascular territory → ARTERY
5. Only then → NAME THE SYNDROME
Don't memorize syndromes as lists; localize the anatomy
