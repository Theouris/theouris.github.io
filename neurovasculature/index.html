Study notes & references

Contralateral means the opposite side of the lesion; ipsilateral means the same side. These are classic localization patterns. Actual deficits depend on the branches involved, anatomical variation, and collateral flow. Dominant hemisphere is usually the left.

The complete Circle of Willis includes the anterior communicating artery, the proximal anterior and posterior cerebral arteries, the posterior communicating arteries, and terminal internal carotid segments. MCA, basilar, and cerebellar arteries are shown nearby but are not part of the ring.
