Which fibers normally synapse there?|Preganglionic parasympathetic fibers from CN III~Sympathetic postganglionic and sensory fibers pass through without using this ganglion as their autonomic synapse.|All sensory fibers from nasociliary nerve~These sensory cell bodies lie in trigeminal ganglion, not ciliary ganglion.|All sympathetic fibers from superior cervical ganglion~These are already postganglionic and do not synapse again in ciliary ganglion.|Retinal ganglion-cell axons~Retinal visual axons travel in II, not through ciliary ganglion as a relay.|All somatic motor fibers to extraocular muscles~These reach skeletal muscles directly rather than synapsing in an autonomic ganglion.
reflex-3|Pupillary lesion localization|A painful new third-nerve palsy includes a dilated poorly reactive pupil. Which vascular lesion is a classic compressive cause to consider in this high-yield localization pattern?|Posterior communicating artery aneurysm~A compressive aneurysm can affect superficial III parasympathetic fibers; pupil findings are useful but do not by themselves exclude serious causes in pupil-sparing presentations.|External carotid stenosis as the classic III compression site~This is not the characteristic intracranial aneurysm association.|Anterior spinal artery occlusion at the lumbar cord~This does not compress III near the midbrain.|Ductus arteriosus persistence~This is not the classic cause of an acute III palsy.|Occlusion of a superficial scalp vein~This does not explain the characteristic compressive III syndrome.
reflex-10|Vestibulo-ocular reflex|A lesion destroys the left abducens nucleus rather than only the left abducens nerve. Which deficit is expected during attempted left gaze?|Both eyes fail to make normal conjugate leftward movement~The nucleus contains left lateral rectus motor neurons and internuclear neurons for right medial rectus via the MLF; a nerve lesion alone mainly impairs left abduction.|Only the left pupil fails to constrict~VI is not the pupil efferent nerve.|Only left corneal sensation is lost~Corneal afference is V1.|Both eyes lose right visual fields~This is a left postchiasmal visual pathway pattern.|Both medial recti are paralyzed during near convergence as an obligatory result~The medial rectus final motor neurons are in III, and convergence may remain available.
reflex-13|Internuclear ophthalmoplegia|A paramedian pontine lesion affects the left horizontal gaze center/abducens nuclear region and the adjacent left MLF. Neither eye moves left; on attempted right gaze, only the right eye abducts. Which syndrome best describes this extension of INO?|Left one-and-a-half syndrome~Ipsilateral gaze palsy plus ipsilateral INO leaves only contralateral abduction as the remaining horizontal movement.|Isolated left optic neuritis~This affects sensory vision, not this combined horizontal motor pattern.|Isolated right VI nerve palsy~That would impair right abduction, the movement preserved here.|Bitemporal hemianopia~This is a chiasmal visual field pattern.|Isolated Adie tonic pupil~This is pupillary parasympathetic dysfunction rather than a conjugate gaze syndrome.
