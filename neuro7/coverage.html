Source qualifications and clinical corrections

  • Auditory/vestibular slide 6: the connexin-26 gene is GJB2, not “GBJ2.” Cochlear implantation bypasses impaired sensory transduction; benefit, age eligibility, and language outcomes depend on individual candidacy and rehabilitation.
  • Auditory/vestibular slide 48: NIOSH recommends 85 dBA over eight hours with a 3-dB exchange rate. OSHA uses a 90-dBA eight-hour permissible exposure limit with a 5-dB exchange rate; 85 dBA is its hearing-conservation action level. These standards are distinguished in the questions.
  • Sudden SNHL is commonly defined as at least 30 dB at three contiguous frequencies within 72 hours. It warrants urgent assessment. Systemic corticosteroids may be offered early, and intratympanic treatment has a salvage role; historical pathology theories do not establish the cause of every idiopathic case.
  • Audiogram severity ranges and asymmetric-hearing referral cutoffs vary by convention and population. Word recognition percentages describe discrimination, not threshold intensity. The additional figures use conventional O/right-air, X/left-air, and angle/bracket bone-conduction symbols; other masked symbols may also appear.
  • Auditory/vestibular slides 54–61: pulse synchrony suggests a vascular source but does not mean an examiner must be able to hear it. Objective audibility and pulsatile character overlap without being synonymous. Patulous and obstructive Eustachian tube dysfunction have different mechanisms.
  • Auditory/vestibular slides 67–70 retain advertisements and historical medication lists as source material. They do not establish supplement efficacy. Persistent primary tinnitus is generally managed through education, audiologic care, appropriate hearing rehabilitation, and behavioral approaches such as CBT. Antidepressants, anticonvulsants, or benzodiazepines are not routine cures solely for primary tinnitus; treat associated conditions when indicated. Gabapentin binds alpha-2-delta calcium-channel subunits rather than acting as a direct GABA receptor agonist. Do not automatically stop indicated low-dose aspirin.
  • Posterior canal BPPV classically produces torsional-upbeating Dix-Hallpike nystagmus. Otoconia originate from the utricle and may be free in a canal or attached to the cupula. The lecture's “geotropic” shorthand is not the complete posterior-canal description.
  • Vestibular neuritis typically spares hearing. Labyrinthitis involves hearing as well as vestibular function; bacterial cases require infection-directed care. Acute hearing loss with vertigo can also be vascular. Focal neurologic findings require consideration of central causes; head movement worsening symptoms alone does not establish BPPV.
  • Vestibular suppressants may help briefly in an acute attack but can hinder compensation if prolonged. Corticosteroid benefit in neuritis is context-dependent. Ondansetron is an antiemetic rather than a direct vestibular suppressant.
  • Meniere disease is associated with endolymphatic hydrops, not defined by one simple universal mechanism. Maintenance diet/drug and sac-surgery evidence varies. Betahistine use/availability differs by country. Dyazide/Maxzide combine hydrochlorothiazide with triamterene. Ablative surgery and intratympanic gentamicin can sacrifice hearing or vestibular function and require careful selection.
  • Ophthalmics slide 4: betaxolol is relatively beta-1 selective, unlike nonselective timolol/carteolol. Ocular beta blockers can still cause systemic bronchospasm, bradycardia, or conduction problems. Medication durations are approximations.
  • Mannitol shifts water from ocular tissues through a plasma osmotic gradient, not exclusively from the anterior chamber; volume, cardiac, and renal effects matter. Rho kinase inhibitors can cause hyperemia despite being described as well tolerated.
  • Acute angle closure requires urgent pressure reduction and definitive treatment of the mechanism. Pilocarpine may not work while extreme pressure leaves the iris ischemic. A drug's listing for glaucoma does not mean it is adequate alone or appropriate for every subtype.
  • Visual/auditory slides 9–12: dark rods are relatively depolarized; light hyperpolarizes them. Transducin is the G protein. Light photoisomerization creates activated pigment; rhodopsin kinase phosphorylation and arrestin terminate activity rather than create the initial active state. ON/OFF bipolar responses differ. Lecture voltages are simplified teaching approximations.
  • Visual/auditory slide 14: the pictured surface patches are Bitot spots, not retinal scotomas. Vitamin A repletion can improve reversible rod dysfunction; route, dose, and recovery time depend on the patient. IV treatment and a minutes-to-hour recovery are not universal rules.
  • Loss of one eye removes binocular stereopsis, but monocular depth cues remain. A retrochiasmal lesion produces a contralateral homonymous field deficit.
  • Visual/auditory slides 26/29–35: Deiters cells are outer phalangeal cells. Basal stiffness/high-frequency and apical compliance/low-frequency are the key tonotopic relationships. Low-frequency waves can move basal regions while peaking apically; the claim that high-pitch discrimination is universally easier is not a general rule. Outer hair bundles are embedded in the tectorial membrane more directly than inner bundles.
  • The lateral lemniscus is an ascending brainstem tract, not solely a midbrain structure. Primary auditory cortex is chiefly BA41 with adjacent auditory BA42. Higher unilateral auditory lesions usually spare basic hearing in both ears while impairing processing/localization; they do not uniformly cause major bilateral threshold suppression.
